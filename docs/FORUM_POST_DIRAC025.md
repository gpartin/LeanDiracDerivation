DIRAC-025 PASS: the electron's velocity spread and its moving clock are the same ratio

We found another exact relationship hiding in the derivation.

The lattice had already given us two separate results: a massive wave moves
slower than the limiting speed, and its internal phase clock slows by the
usual relativistic factor. DIRAC-025 now proves those are not merely two
parallel formulas.

Using the velocity operation already derived from the Hamiltonian, Lean finds
that its full squared value is always the limiting speed squared. But a
positive-energy massive state has a smaller average velocity. The difference
between those two quantities is its velocity spread. The surprising result is

    velocity spread / limiting speed
      = moving clock rate / rest clock rate
      = sqrt(1 - speed^2 / limiting speed^2)

So the exact Pythagorean identity is

    (speed / limiting speed)^2 + (velocity spread / limiting speed)^2 = 1

The familiar numbers fall straight out. At 60% of the limiting speed the
clock and velocity-spread ratio are 80%. At 80% speed they are 60%. At
86.6025% speed they are exactly 50%.

Nothing was tuned to obtain those ratios. The velocity operator came from the
existing momentum response, the average came from the existing current, and
the clock came independently from the phase accumulated along that current.
Lean then proved they are exactly the same factor for every nonzero
three-dimensional momentum and both positive-energy spin states.

An independent matrix reconstruction reproduced the theorem to about
`1.4e-14` and rejected an unnormalized direction, a transverse direction and
the wrong rest mass. No action term, field, coefficient, speed rule or clock
rule was added.

This does not say the electron follows a hidden classical zig-zag path. What
it does say is that, inside the same derived Dirac sector, the amount by which
the average motion falls short of the limiting velocity is encoded by exactly
the same ratio that slows the moving internal clock.
