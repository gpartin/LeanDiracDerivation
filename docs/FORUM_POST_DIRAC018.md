DIRAC-018 now passes in our Lean LFM-to-Dirac chain.

The question was whether our cubical Dirac square root had been chosen by hand. Within the precise class of real, local, first-order, flavor-preserving operators made from the cube's own incidence directions, Lean proves it is forced: cube symmetry makes all three direction coefficients equal, the required square fixes their size, and the positive mass/current orientation derived earlier fixes the remaining sign. This is proved independently for both exact four-component copies, which together exhaust the eight-corner state.

We also added two important controls. Reversing the whole operator still gives the same square but violates the established orientation; reflecting only one axis still gives the same square but breaks cube symmetry. The exact scalar C19 transport symbol also has no extra zero inside the first Brillouin zone.

This does not prove uniqueness among arbitrary nonlocal or copy-mixing operators, an exact finite-spacing Dirac operator, a universal solution to fermion doubling, or a physical electron. No field or action term was added. Next we will test whether an exact first-order factorization of the complete finite-lattice C19 symbol exists, or prove a carefully scoped obstruction.
